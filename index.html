How I define the players

There is no official list of “Process players,” and reasonable fans would draw the line in different places. I wanted a definition that is accurate to the era and that can be built entirely from open data (rosters, stats and draft history) rather than a hand-curated list. So the rules are simple, and they work the same way for every season.

Process Player

Any player on the Sixers' roster during the Hinkie era, from the 2013-14 season through 2016-17.

I also include the first round of the 2017 draft. Colangelo made those picks, but it was Hinkie's plan that acquired them.

Once a player qualifies, he counts as a Process player in every season he appears, which is what lets the ship drain as they leave.

Hinkie Draft Night Acquisition

A highlight within the Process group. One big motivation for tanking was the best possible lottery odds, so I mark the players Hinkie brought in on draft night: Sixers draft picks from 2013, 2014 and 2015, plus two draft-night trades the draft data can't see (Nerlens Noel and Dario Saric).

Because of this definition, the 2016 and 2017 draftees count as Process players but don't get the gold ring.

Later Player

Any player who arrives in the 2017-18 season or later, other than the Sixers' 2017 first-round picks.

They are the replacement planks in the ship.

Where the data comes from

Everything here is pulled automatically from the NBA's public stats site with the nba_api Python library (pull_data.py). The only hand-entered detail is two draft-night trades, noted below (as well as on the prior slide).

What comes from where

  • Player stats (games, minutes, points, rebounds, assists): the league player stats feed, Sixers only, regular season, per game.
  • Rosters and jersey numbers: each season's final team roster. Players who were on the roster but never played show up as “did not play.” Jersey numbers come from this same list (see below).
  • Win–loss records (and the trendline): the team's year-by-year totals.
  • Draft details (year, round, pick, and the team that made the pick): the league's draft history.
  • Headshots: the NBA's headshot image server. Initials appear when a photo is missing.

Good to know

  • Jersey numbers. The roster feed only lists who was on the team at the end of a season, so players traded or waived mid-season have no number there. A card with a number means that player was on the Sixers' roster when the season ended, and a card without one means he left before then.
  • Draft-night trades. Draft data names the team that made the pick, so Nerlens Noel and Dario Saric (acquired on draft night) are marked as Hinkie draft-night acquisitions by hand.
  • Sixers time only. A player's stats count only his games with the Sixers that season. Playoffs are not included.
